# Protective Puts

A protective put is a hedging strategy in which an investor buys put options to insure against downside risk in an underlying position, providing a defined floor below which losses cannot exceed. While puts offer genuine protection during market crises, backtesting over 19.5 years shows that perpetual hedging is economically expensive, costing 2.9–5.1% annually due to put skew, rolling costs, and the shifting deductible problem. Rather than buying and holding puts indefinitely, practitioners can improve risk-adjusted returns through scenario-based hedging, dynamic scaling in and out of protection, or diversified strategy construction that balances protection costs against portfolio objectives.
