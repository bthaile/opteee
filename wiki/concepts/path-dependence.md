# Path Dependence

Path dependence in options trading refers to how a position's value and behavior between entry and expiration differs from its terminal (expiration) value, requiring active management as market conditions and underlying prices evolve. Unlike strategies focused solely on final P&L, path-dependent analysis acknowledges that Greeks—particularly gamma and theta—create velocity and mark-to-market dynamics that force traders to reconcile their initial thesis with real-time position deltas and changing conviction. This concept is especially critical in short-duration instruments like 0DTE options, where gamma amplification and rapid decay create path-dependent traps for unprepared traders, and in leveraged or concentrated positions where path volatility can trigger ruin before a thesis proves correct. Effective path-dependent trading requires active rebalancing, honest position management, and acceptance that ignoring intermediate dynamics—even with an attractive terminal risk-reward—can destroy capital through drawdown or forced liquidation.
