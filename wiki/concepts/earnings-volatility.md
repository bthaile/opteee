# Earnings Volatility

Earnings volatility refers to the elevated implied volatility that markets price into options around company earnings announcements, reflecting uncertainty about post-earnings price moves. Traders use the volatility surface—comparing implied volatility across strikes and expirations—to identify whether earnings-related volatility is expensive or cheap relative to historical realized volatility, enabling decisions about whether to sell premium (straddles and strangles) or buy protection. The term structure of volatility leading into earnings, including skew patterns and percentile rankings of implied volatility, provides signals about market positioning and directional bias that inform trade construction and timing.
