# Directional Bias

Directional bias refers to a trader's conviction about the direction a market or stock will move, and it must be matched to the appropriate options structure to capture profit effectively. The critical mistake is selecting a strategy whose payoff profile contradicts the directional thesis—for example, using a capped-upside structure when expecting unlimited upside movement. Proper trade construction begins by identifying the profit mechanism or edge first, then filtering through questions about location, width, shape, and term to determine which options structure (vertical spread, long call, straddle, etc.) best expresses that directional view while aligning Greeks and risk profile to the intended holding period and conviction level.
