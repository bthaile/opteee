# Drawdown Management

Drawdown management in options trading refers to the practice of controlling and reducing the magnitude of peak-to-trough losses through position sizing, entry discipline, hedging techniques, and strategic structure selection. Across various strategies—from selling premium in low-VIX environments to sector rotation and the wheel strategy—proper drawdown management involves setting entry gates, adjusting time-to-expiration, using delta hedging, and modifying coverage ratios to balance risk-adjusted returns against catastrophic losses. The data shows that even sound underlying theses can produce drawdowns exceeding 40% without careful position construction, and that reducing maximum drawdown often requires accepting some trade-off in terminal returns or upside capture. Successful traders emphasize mechanical optimization and capital efficiency as core ingredients for long-term survival, treating drawdown control as inseparable from win-rate and position-sizing discipline.
