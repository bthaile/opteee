# Drawdown Protection

Drawdown protection in options trading refers to strategies that limit portfolio losses during market declines, most commonly through the purchase of protective put options. While long puts provide genuine downside cushioning during crises, backtests spanning nearly two decades reveal that perpetual put hedging erodes returns substantially through premium costs, theta decay, and rolling expenses—typically 2.9–5.1% annually—often outweighing the protection gained except during severe market stress. Rather than carrying continuous hedges, traders can employ alternatives such as timing put purchases to specific risk scenarios, dynamically scaling position size down during weakness and back up during recovery, or using delta-hedging frameworks with rebalancing bands to manage costs more efficiently. The core tension is that hedging functions like insurance: it provides real protection but at a measurable cost that compounds over time, requiring traders to weigh tail-risk mitigation against the drag on long-term returns.
