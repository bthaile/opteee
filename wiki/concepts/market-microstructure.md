# Market Microstructure

Market microstructure refers to the mechanics of how options markets function at granular levels—including bid-ask spreads, liquidity provision, order routing, payment for order flow, and the role of market makers in price discovery and execution. Understanding market microstructure is essential for options traders because it directly affects execution quality, the true cost of trading, and how structural flows (such as retail order concentration or zero-DTE proliferation) can create both opportunities and hidden risks. The channel emphasizes that microstructure dynamics vary significantly across trader scales and exchange types (standard listed options versus FLEX options, for instance), and that misconceptions about liquidity and gamma exposure often stem from incomplete understanding of how orders are actually processed and priced in real markets.
