# Volatility Crush

Volatility crush occurs when implied volatility (IV) contracts sharply, typically after an event such as earnings or an FOMC announcement, causing option prices to decline even if the underlying asset moves favorably. Traders who buy options before such events face the dual risk of directional loss and IV compression, while those who sell volatility benefit from the crush but must manage the concentrated gamma risk that precedes it. Understanding volatility term structure and the variance risk premium—rather than relying solely on IV percentile or absolute IV levels—is critical for properly timing volatility sales and avoiding the structural imbalances that can trap traders in low-VIX environments.
