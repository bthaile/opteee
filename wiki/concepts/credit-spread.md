# Credit Spread

A credit spread is a short-premium strategy that sells an option and buys a protective wing at a wider strike to define maximum loss, commonly used as a foundational approach for traders seeking defined risk. Despite their popularity and often-high win rates (62–97%), empirical analysis shows that 89% of credit spread configurations lose money over time due to negative expected value masked by frequent small wins and occasional catastrophic losses. The strategy's profitability depends critically on underlying edge—such as favorable fill quality, appropriate strike selection, and disciplined position sizing—rather than risk definition alone, which merely caps losses but does not guarantee positive returns. Bid-ask spread costs and aggressive management rules further erode edge, making credit spreads viable only when combined with systematic entry criteria, realistic position sizing relative to account size, and alignment between trade duration and market regime.
