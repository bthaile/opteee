# Time Decay

Time decay, measured by theta, is the rate at which an option loses extrinsic value as expiration approaches, and it accelerates nonlinearly in the final days before expiration. In options trading, time decay is a core profit mechanism for short premium strategies (like covered strangles) and a cost for long option buyers, making it essential to align position structure and holding period with directional conviction and edge. The relationship between time decay and other Greeks—particularly gamma and delta—becomes increasingly volatile near expiration, especially in zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE) options, requiring active management and awareness of path-dependency rather than passive premium collection.
