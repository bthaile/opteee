# Black Scholes

The Black-Scholes-Merton model is an option pricing framework that takes five primary inputs—underlying price, strike, time to expiration, risk-free rate, and volatility—to calculate theoretical option values, with volatility being the only truly unknowable input since it represents a forecast of future realized volatility. While the model provides a mathematical foundation for options trading, its real-world application contains hidden complexities: time does not pass uniformly across calendar days or intraday periods, yet standard implementations treat all days equally, creating systematic measurement artifacts that can distort implied volatility readings, particularly near expiration and across weekends. Understanding both the model's mechanics and its practical limitations—especially how to properly measure volatility time—is essential for traders seeking to identify genuine market opportunities rather than model artifacts.
