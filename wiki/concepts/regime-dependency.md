# Regime Dependency

Regime dependency refers to the observation that trading strategies and market mechanisms perform differently depending on the underlying market environment—such as volatility regimes, trend conditions, or structural market states—and that ignoring these shifts can cause even positive-expectancy strategies to fail catastrophically. The concept appears across multiple contexts: volatility-based strategies may collapse when regime assumptions break, momentum strategies require explicit regime filters (such as moving-average thresholds) to distinguish bullish from bearish environments, and high win-rate tactics can mask tail risk that emerges only in adverse regimes. Proper strategy design therefore demands stress-testing across historical regime changes, understanding how profit mechanisms behave in different market states, and sizing positions conservatively enough to survive regime transitions without ruin.
