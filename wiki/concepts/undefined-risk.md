# Undefined Risk

Undefined risk refers to positions where the maximum potential loss is theoretically unlimited or cannot be precisely calculated before entry, typically occurring when selling naked options without protective wings or hedges. Common examples include short strangles and other short premium strategies that offer higher probability of profit but expose traders to catastrophic losses if the market moves sharply against the position. Unlike defined-risk strategies such as iron condors or vertical spreads, undefined-risk positions require active management, strict position sizing, and disciplined exit rules rather than relying on a predetermined maximum loss. The choice between defined and undefined risk depends on a trader's risk tolerance, capital, and willingness to monitor positions actively, with the tradeoff being that undefined-risk strategies can generate superior returns when managed properly but demand greater skill and psychological discipline.
