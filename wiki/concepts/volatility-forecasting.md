# Volatility Forecasting

Volatility forecasting is the practice of predicting future price fluctuations to inform options trading decisions, encompassing techniques ranging from foundational approaches like standard deviation and realized volatility to advanced statistical models such as GARCH and HAR that analyze tick-level data. Since volatility is the primary driver of option prices, accurate forecasting enables traders to identify edge—a statistical advantage—before selecting specific instruments or strategies. The discipline requires distinguishing between historical volatility, realized volatility, implied volatility, and forward-looking estimates, and avoiding common mistakes such as relying on mechanical models that compete with professional pricing or confusing descriptive market observations with predictive signals. Effective volatility forecasting prioritizes finding genuine edge over optimizing mechanics, and practitioners often benefit from selling volatility around defined events rather than attempting to outpredict sophisticated institutional models.
