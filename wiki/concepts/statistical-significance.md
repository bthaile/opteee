# Statistical Significance

Statistical significance in options trading refers to the ability to distinguish whether observed trading results or correlations reflect genuine, repeatable patterns or merely random noise—a distinction that determines whether a strategy has true edge or is merely gambling. Traders establish statistical significance by identifying a repeatable edge, managing position sizing appropriately, and collecting enough samples across varying market regimes to confirm the edge survives path dependence and regime shifts. The concept is central to validating trading strategies: apparent correlations (such as between gamma exposure and market range) often collapse when controlling for confounding variables like implied volatility, and surface-level returns can mask whether outperformance stems from skill, favorable regime conditions, or luck. Rigorous statistical evaluation requires moving beyond intuitive observations to systematic research that isolates true causal drivers and ensures a strategy's profitability persists across sufficient independent trials.
