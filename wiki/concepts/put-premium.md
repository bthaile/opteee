# Put Premium

Put premium refers to the price paid to purchase put options, which provide downside protection but carry significant costs that accumulate over time. Long-term hedging with puts is expensive due to put skew (the pricing premium for downside protection), rolling costs, and the shifting deductible problem, making perpetual downside protection economically unviable for most portfolios over extended periods. Traders often finance put purchases by selling calls against them in collar structures, illustrating the fundamental trade-off between buying downside protection and sacrificing upside capture. The cost of put premium extends beyond the initial purchase to include opportunity costs and the persistent drag from rolling protection repeatedly as market conditions change.
