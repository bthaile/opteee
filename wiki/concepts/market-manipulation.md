# Market Manipulation

Market manipulation in options trading refers to illegal or structurally exploitative practices such as spoofing (placing fake orders to move prices), naked short-selling, and coordinated hedging dynamics that artificially influence stock and options prices. The concept encompasses both deliberate fraud schemes prosecuted by regulators and structural market microstructure issues—such as continuous trading versus batch auctions and settlement mechanics—that enable manipulation at scale. In the context of GameStop and meme stocks, market manipulation allegations have been leveled against various parties including market makers and hedge funds, while the distinction between legitimate hedging cascades from options positioning and actual manipulation remains contested between legal definitions and practical market mechanics.
