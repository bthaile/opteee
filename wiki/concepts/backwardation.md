# Backwardation

Backwardation in options markets refers to a term structure where near-term implied volatility is higher than longer-dated implied volatility, the opposite of contango. This condition appears in the volatility surface across different expiration dates and can signal market stress, positioning imbalances, or anticipated near-term events such as earnings; traders monitor backwardated term structures to identify trading opportunities and understand whether volatility expectations are concentrated in the front month or spread across longer periods. Backwardation is particularly relevant when analyzing gamma ramps and dealer hedging dynamics, as clustering of options activity in near-term strikes with elevated call skew can create backwardated conditions that amplify price moves.
