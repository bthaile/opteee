# Downside Protection

Downside protection refers to strategies and instruments used to limit losses during market declines, most commonly implemented through buying put options or constructing collar strategies that combine long puts with short calls. A collar reduces downside protection costs by financing a long put through call premium, capping both losses below a strike price and gains above another, creating an asymmetric payoff that sacrifices some upside to preserve capital during severe drawdowns. Market participants systematically overpay for downside protection through put skew, reflecting the reality that downside moves are faster and more violent than upside moves, making puts an expensive but valuable insurance tool. The trade-off between downside protection and returns is fundamental: while hedging reduces maximum drawdown and recovery time after systemic shocks, it also caps total wealth accumulation compared to unhedged buy-and-hold approaches.
