# Hedge

A hedge is a risk-management strategy that reduces downside losses in a portfolio, typically through options like protective puts or collars, but always involves a cost—either in premium paid or upside potential sacrificed. Common misconceptions frame strategies like selling calls or covered calls as hedges, but they provide minimal downside protection while significantly limiting gains, making them poor hedging tools despite other potential merits. Effective hedges must be evaluated across multiple dimensions including maximum drawdown reduction, worst-year performance, terminal wealth impact, and opportunity cost to determine whether the protection justifies its expense. The core tension in hedging is that peak profitability of protective positions often coincides with maximum market stress, creating a dilemma between realizing gains and maintaining needed protection.
