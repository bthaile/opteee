# Drawdown Risk

Drawdown risk refers to the peak-to-trough decline in a trading account's equity and represents the largest loss a trader may experience during normal market operation. Managing drawdown risk requires establishing a pre-defined account-size-based risk budget and implementing circuit breakers that automatically trigger position cuts when drawdown thresholds are breached, signaling an unaccounted-for market regime change. Position sizing is the primary lever for controlling drawdown outcomes before trades execute; identical strategies sized at different risk levels produce vastly different equity curves and drawdown profiles, making sizing decisions as critical as strategy selection itself. Proper drawdown management acknowledges that tail risk is real and compensates traders for bearing it, but over-hedging for worst-case scenarios can prevent meaningful capital deployment and returns.
