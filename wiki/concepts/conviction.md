# Conviction

Conviction in options trading refers to a trader's confidence level in a directional thesis, which directly shapes position structure and sizing decisions. High-conviction trades typically involve concentrated, leveraged positions held over extended periods without fixed profit targets, while lower-conviction setups call for more conservative Greeks alignment and risk management through spreads. Conviction can be assessed through multiple lenses—statistical strength of a signal, options flow patterns that suggest informed positioning versus directional betting, and a trader's willingness to tolerate significant portfolio volatility. Traders with strong conviction often employ strategies like buying far out-of-the-money calls on conviction names and converting profitable positions into defined-risk spreads, managing these bets with patience and proper position sizing rather than mechanical price targets.
