# The Costly Reality of Put Hedging

## Summary

A 19.5-year backtest of long-put hedging strategies on SPY reveals that the cost of downside protection via put options is substantially higher than most traders anticipate. The analysis examines nine variations of put hedging across all market regimes from 2007 to 2026, demonstrating that while risk reduction sounds appealing, the premium paid for insurance erodes returns significantly.

## Key takeaways

- **Backtest scope**: 19.5 years of SPY data (2007–2026) with nine put-hedging variations to capture behavior across vastly different market conditions [00:00]
- **Core finding**: The cost of put-based hedging is "absolutely brutal" — insurance via options premium is expensive relative to the protection gained [00:00]
- **Risk-reduction paradox**: While reducing risk sounds universally desirable, the mechanism (buying puts) carries a high carry cost that compounds over time [00:00]
- **Hedge-cost trade-off**: The premium paid for downside protection must be weighed against opportunity cost and drag on long-term returns [00:00]

## Regime / context

This backtest spans multiple market cycles including the 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 COVID crash, and the 2022 bear market, providing a comprehensive view of put-hedging performance across bull, bear, and sideways regimes. The analysis is dated October 2026.
