# 39K & Earnings! | Live Stock Market Analysis

## Summary

Eric celebrates the channel reaching 39,000 YouTube subscribers and provides full transparency on platform metrics, ad revenue (~$9.51/hour lifetime), and audience demographics. The second half pivots to Q2 2026 earnings season analysis, showing a 79% beat rate across 78 reporters with 6.7% median EPS surprise; he demonstrates filtering techniques for earnings trades and discusses how guidance dominates post-earnings stock reactions.

## Key takeaways

### Dated market read (2026-07-23)

- **Earnings season snapshot** [31:55]: 62 of 78 reporters have beaten (79% beat rate); median EPS surprise is 6.7%; 59% of moves stayed inside the straddle print. Over the last 90 days, 2,276 reporters show 66.5% beat rate with 5.9% median surprise.
- **Sector performance** [33:00]: Financials 90% beat (n=20); Consumer Defensive 100% beat (n=8); Materials only 43% beat but still up 6.8% realized move—showing expectations vs. realized divergence.
- **Largest surprises** [34:16]: TRV +89% EPS surprise; Nike massive beat with +3.8% move; MEI +37.5% realized move vs. 20% expected (guidance-driven rally despite miss).
- **Short-vol mechanics** [37:46]: 60% of moves stayed within implied straddle; MEI example shows even large misses can be manageable if filtered correctly.

### Evergreen mechanics

- **Channel transparency & monetization** [36:01]: Lifetime YouTube revenue $349.91 across 1,228 videos and 774 live streams (~$9.51/hour); 2026 YTD revenue $4,526 across 70 videos and 51 live streams (~$20.60/hour). Ad revenue is not the business model; community and teaching are the primary drivers.
- **Audience demographics** [24:32–29:20]: 56.5% new viewers; 32% casual (watched within 5 months); 12% regular (6+ months). Peak watch times: Sunday–Monday 7am–2pm PT, Friday 1–3pm PT. 48% mobile, 92% male-dominated. Age density 25–54 (75%+). Primarily US, with Canada, UK, India, Australia, Germany, Singapore as secondary markets.
- **Community-first positioning** [15:41–16:22]: No paid advertising or engagement farming; community finds channel through word-of-mouth and sharing. Deliberately targets people seeking "real peak behind the curtain" rather than "fast easy money" promises.
- **Earnings filtering & trade selection** [38:52–40:18]: Use historical earnings trends (expected vs. realized move, guidance impact) to filter which tickers to trade. MEI example: despite large realized move, historical volatility patterns show it can move 3–4x expected; therefore avoid for short-vol strategies. Guidance is the dominant post-earnings driver, not EPS beat/miss alone.
- **Position sizing & risk management** [09:34–09:45]: SpaceX short trade example shows ~1% portfolio risk with tight stops; synthetic short vs. outright short comparison reveals different Greeks behavior (theta decay, vega exposure) over time.
- **Discretionary vs. systematic** [12:22–12:52]: Eric emphasizes subconscious processing and "gut feel" in trade decisions; despite statistical rigor, he remains discretionary by design. Paper-trading multiple structures simultaneously to isolate pure behavioral data without research pollution.
