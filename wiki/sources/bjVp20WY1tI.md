# Your SPY Hedge is Profitable, Now What?

## Summary

This analysis examines the paradox of profitable hedges: when protective puts on SPY show significant unrealized gains, the decision to realize those profits creates an immediate unhedged state, reintroducing the tail risk the hedge was designed to mitigate. The core tension is that peak hedge profitability often coincides with maximum market stress—precisely when you need the protection most.

## Key takeaways

- **Hedge profitability paradox** [00:00]: Of 78 quarterly SPY put hedges analyzed, 53 showed profit at some point before expiration, but only 8 actually paid at expiration—illustrating the difficulty of timing hedge realization.
- **Extreme tail scenarios** [00:00]: During COVID Q1 2020, certain hedges delivered ~30X returns, but these outsized gains occurred during the exact market dislocations when the hedge was most valuable as insurance.
- **Unhedged risk upon exit** [00:00]: Selling a profitable hedge to lock in gains immediately removes your downside protection, reintroducing the concentrated risk you paid to offset.
- **Timing and balance** [00:00]: Achieving the "delicate balance" between capturing hedge profits and maintaining portfolio protection requires a disciplined framework—not a reactive, profit-taking approach.

## Notable quotes

> "Most of the time when your return shows the highest is when you need the hedge the most because as soon as you sell this, you are now unhedged."

## Regime / context

This analysis reflects a structural challenge in portfolio hedging: the cost of insurance (premium paid) is often viewed as "dead money" when markets remain calm, yet the highest returns on that insurance occur during crisis periods when the hedge is most needed operationally. The tension between realizing profits and maintaining protection is a perennial portfolio management problem, especially relevant in low-volatility regimes where hedges appear expensive and are frequently abandoned just before drawdowns.
