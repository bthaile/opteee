# Two Critical Reasons Why We Can't Use GEX For Predictions

## Summary

Gamma exposure (GEX) cannot reliably predict market moves because its two critical inputs—dealer long/short positioning in options and actual dealer hedging behavior—are unobservable. The methodology assumes a specific hedging regime that may not reflect real-world dealer practices, creating a fundamental gap between theory and application.

## Key takeaways

- **Unobservable dealer positioning** [00:00] — The sign of dealer gamma (whether dealers are net long or short options) is not publicly visible, making it impossible to know the true direction of GEX pressure.
- **Hedging behavior is the missing link** [00:00] — GEX predictions depend entirely on *how* dealers actually hedge their gamma exposure, but this behavior varies and is not standardized across market participants.
- **Methodology vs. reality gap** [00:00] — GEX models require dealers to hedge in a specific way for the framework to work, but real-world hedging practices often diverge from these assumptions, invalidating predictions.
