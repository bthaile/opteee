# How the Market Prices Options Through Distributions

## Summary

The market prices options by embedding four key dimensions into the probability distribution of an underlying asset: location (directional expectation), width (volatility), shape (skew and asymmetry), and term (expiration-dependent variation). Understanding these components reveals how option premiums reflect not just directional bets but also volatility claims and tail-risk asymmetries.

## Key takeaways

- **Location (directional claim)** [00:00] — The center of the distribution represents the market's consensus directional view; this is the baseline expectation for where the underlying will settle.
- **Width (volatility claim)** [00:00] — The spread of the distribution encodes volatility expectations; wider distributions imply higher implied volatility and greater uncertainty.
- **Shape (skew and asymmetry)** [00:00] — Asymmetry in the distribution, such as put skew (fatter left tail), reflects market pricing of tail risk and directional bias; this is distinct from location and width.
- **Term (expiration structure)** [00:00] — Different expirations have different distributions; the term dimension captures how expectations and volatility evolve across the calendar.
