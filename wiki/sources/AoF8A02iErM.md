# You Can Be 100% Correct on Your Trade Idea and Still Lose Money: This Is Why

## Summary

A directionally correct thesis does not guarantee profitability if the underlying volatility assumptions are misaligned with market pricing. This video demonstrates how to identify and eliminate unintended volatility exposure by mapping trade ideas against a two-axis framework (implied volatility vs. realized volatility), and how to restructure positions—such as converting a long call to a synthetic long—when volatility edge is absent.

## Key takeaways

- **Directional correctness ≠ profitability** [00:00] — You can be right on direction and still lose money if volatility assumptions are wrong.
- **IV vs. RV quadrant framework** [00:00] — Plot implied volatility (X-axis) against expected realized volatility (Y-axis) to identify whether your trade has a volatility edge.
- **No volatility edge = restructure** [00:00] — If IV is high and you expect high RV (no edge), don't take a long call; instead, use a synthetic long (buy call + sell put at same strike) to isolate directional exposure and eliminate volatility drag.
- **Volatility-indifferent positioning** [00:00] — When volatility is not your edge, remove it from the trade structure to avoid paying for premium you won't capture.
