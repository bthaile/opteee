# Is Low VIX a Premium Trap?

## Summary

This analysis examines whether low VIX environments represent a trap for premium sellers. The video challenges the common narrative that sub-16 VIX readings are rare or anomalous, demonstrating that the VIX spends substantial calendar time in low regimes and that calm bands are statistically normal rather than exceptional. The key insight is that when VIX enters sub-13 territory, it tends to persist in that band through expiration approximately 60% of the time using 30-day options.

## Key takeaways

- **VIX distribution is misunderstood** [00:00–00:30]: The VIX spends a lot of its time sub-16, not the majority but a significant portion. Low volatility regimes are not rare anomalies but rather common market states.

- **Calm bands are statistically normal** [00:30–01:00]: Depending on where you draw the threshold, calm volatility bands represent most of the calendar. The market generally spends a lot of time in unremarkable locations rather than extremes.

- **Sub-13 VIX persistence** [01:00–01:30]: When VIX drops below 13, it remains in that band approximately 60% of the time through 30-day option expiration, suggesting mean-reversion dynamics are not as aggressive as premium sellers might assume.

- **Regime persistence over mean reversion** [01:30–end]: The data implies that low-volatility regimes exhibit stickiness; premium sellers should not assume automatic reversion to higher levels simply because VIX is at historical lows.
