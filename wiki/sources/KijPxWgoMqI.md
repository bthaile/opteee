# Tom Sosnoff: Why Most Option Strategies Fail (And How to Fix Yours) | Unhedged

## Summary

Tom Sosnoff discusses the evolution of his approach to options trading, emphasizing that successful strategies are built on mechanical optimization and capital efficiency rather than theoretical edge. He outlines the core ingredients underlying all trading strategies, advocates for high-probability, high-win-rate approaches (particularly [[short-put]] and [[covered-call]]), and stresses the critical role of decision-making velocity and position sizing in long-term trader survival.

## Key takeaways

### Strategy fundamentals
- **Capital efficiency is the primary driver** [02:22–03:42]: Most retail traders face capital constraints; strategies must be optimized to trade expensive underlyings (e.g., $1,100 Micron) with limited account size (e.g., $40–50k).
- **All strategies are variations on ~5–6 core ingredients** [01:10–02:22]: Like Taco Bell's five ingredients, the financial ecosystem recombines the same basic building blocks; the edge lies in optimization, not novelty.
- **Mechanical optimization over theoretical edge** [00:00–01:10]: Modern success comes from optimizing mechanics (duration, delta, IV context, entry/exit timing) rather than claiming a theoretical advantage.

### Beginner-friendly strategies
- **Short naked puts: highest probability trade** [20:41–21:47]: Positive drift, highest win rate on the board, especially in high-IV environments; capital-efficient sister to covered calls.
- **Covered calls: higher capital requirement, lower probability** [21:47–22:44]: Same risk profile as naked puts but uses more capital; less efficient for retail.
- **Vertical spreads: capital efficiency via defined risk** [22:44]: Simplest defined-risk structure; use expected move to set short strike, adjust long strike based on conviction.

### Portfolio construction
- **Tom's personal allocation** [22:44–24:10]: ~50% naked strangles (short put + call), ~20% short puts, ~10% verticals; evolved from naked single-side options toward selling both sides.
- **Position management by capital usage and delta** [24:10–26:32]: Monitor buying power, net liquidating value, delta, and theta daily; diversify across ~70 underlyings; maintain non-correlation via futures and equity mix.
- **Strike selection discipline** [27:59–29:05]: Set short strike at expected move; tweak based on conviction (bullish/bearish bias); avoid arbitrary strike-picking based on credit received.

### Discretion within rules
- **Contrarian positioning as core philosophy** [29:05–30:08]: Take opposite side of consensus; sell puts in beaten-down stocks (Netflix, Uber, IBM), sell call spreads in runup names (Micron, Meta, Nasdaq); driven by "what have you done lately," not mean-reversion dogma.
- **Intuition from monitoring and experience** [30:08–31:13]: Build contrarian instinct through years of trade observation; recognize that nobody knows tomorrow's price, so act on relative positioning, not prediction.

### Risk and sizing
- **Law of large numbers vs. blowup risk** [33:15–34:31]: Staying power comes from belief in law of large numbers and managed outcomes; stepping up size to "take the big shot" triggers genius-fails scenarios (When Genius Failed).
- **Small-account reality** [34:31–36:54]: Turning $5k into $5M is rare; focus on learning financial strategy, probabilistic thinking, and decision-making; use small position sizes across many underlyings to build skill.
- **Decision velocity as wealth driver** [38:00–40:05]: Success correlates with speed and frequency of decisions, not IQ; people with smaller accounts often made fewer risky decisions earlier in life; every positive-expected-value opportunity should be taken.

### Decision-making practice
- **Trade volume as decision-making training** [41:11–42:18]: University of Chicago study: ten eBay trades improve economic success tenfold; apply to markets: make 1,000 one-lot vertical spreads or micro-futures trades to become 1,000× better at decision-making.
- **Reframe small-account trading as education** [43:30–44:23]: College students: scrape up $2k for Vegas? Use it for 100 trades instead; build trajectory through volume, not dollar outcomes.

### AI and technology
- **AI as optimization and discipline tool** [46:31–50:07]: AI helps optimize mechanics, monitor outlier risk, reduce content creation time, accelerate software releases; no downside (can be turned off); levels playing field and reduces fees.
- **Heavy internal adoption**: Every team member at Tom's firm runs out of AI tokens daily; AI touches every part of business without eliminating jobs.
