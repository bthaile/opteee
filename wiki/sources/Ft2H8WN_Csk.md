# When an Options Trade Goes Wrong: Tom Sosnoff's Strategy

## Summary

Tom Sosnoff walks through a live trade adjustment in Boeing after a $17 intraday move against his short 165 put / 180 call strangle, discussing mechanical adjustment protocols, scenario planning for further downside, and the philosophy behind premium selling. The episode covers loss recovery mechanics, position sizing discipline, and broader market themes including bond positioning, commodity trades, and the upcoming Anthropic IPO.

## Key takeaways

### Trade adjustment mechanics
- **Boeing strangle setup** [01:06]: Sold 165 November puts at ~85¢ and 180 calls at ~196¢; stock dropped $17 intraday, puts moved to 365¢, calls to 150–158¢. Current loss ~$230–235 per contract.
- **Immediate call adjustment** [03:06]: Sold 180 calls with ~1 hour to close, saving ~40¢ and reducing immediate risk.
- **Put-side roll discipline** [04:18]: Will not roll puts until Friday/Monday; if stock stays above 165, will roll out to December to reduce delta by 20–25%. If stock approaches 165, will roll 180 calls down to 175 or 170.
- **Downside scenario protocol** [05:37]: If stock drops another 10%, will roll calls down aggressively first, then roll out to December. Will NOT add delta or double up position.
- **Upside scenario** [06:32]: If stock rallies $7, will roll puts up 2.5–5 strikes to stay long delta and avoid "double whammy."
- **Break-even target** [08:33]: Goal is always to get close to break-even on a losing trade, not to recover full profit; keep the game close until end.
- **IV rank exit rule** [09:28]: If IV rank drops to single digits (currently ~25), will exit and take losses rather than endure prolonged torture.

### Robinhood earnings trade
- **Setup and context** [11:22]: Sold October 85 puts at 85¢ when stock was at 94.5–95; stock down ~$5 after close. Expected move is ~$8, so puts are still within expected move.
- **Conviction difference** [13:24]: Unlike Boeing (no conviction, pure trade), Sosnoff likes Robinhood as a company and has traded it all year; willing to carry more delta here.
- **Probable scenario**: Hoping for stock to open at 91–93 to eke out small profit; may need damage control Friday.

### Broader positioning and philosophy
- **Lost Dog platform launch** [14:28]: New earnings platform launching next week with historical expected-move data and conditional analysis; also launching "One Lucky Dog" ideas app (Friday–Tuesday pending App Store approval).
- **"Nobody knows" thesis** [16:45]: Built app around principle that no one—not even CEOs—can predict market direction; focus on mechanics and edge, not prediction. Cites story of legendary Chicago trader who said "Don't ever tell me what you think the market's going to do."
- **Thesis vs. knowledge** [24:03]: Differentiates between building a conviction/thesis (fine) and claiming to "know" the market. Michael Burry built a thesis on housing; Sosnoff makes many calls, occasionally right, but doesn't "know" anything.
- **GFC decision-making** [26:04]: E*TRADE nearly failed because they laddered credit default swaps for 200 bps over risk-free rates; Sosnoff and partner Scott declined, viewing it as uncompensated principal risk. Lived through '87, '89, 2000–2002 (Nasdaq -80%), 2008–2009 (S&P to 666) crashes, so risk-aware.
- **Anthropic IPO** [36:55]: Thinks stock is fully priced at 1.7–2 trillion valuation; would not participate at high end of range. Expects opening around 95–105, then decline to ~55–60 (similar to SpaceX trajectory). Will sell volatility and delta (20–22 delta calls vs. 16 delta puts) on day one.

### Commodity and macro positioning
- **Bonds** [43:41]: Bullish on bonds; at 107.5, Fed will hold rates unchanged at FOMC. Bottom is in for bonds; will buy any dip. Expects 10-year to stay 4.75–5.10 for a long time.
- **Yield curve** [45:52]: Likes 30-10 spread (1 ZB to 2 ZN ratio); too narrow now but will trade on extremes. Doesn't like 2-10 or 5-10.
- **Commodities** [46:57]: Long gold and silver (short puts); short calls in crude oil; short strangles in natural gas (~20 delta, call skew favors far OTM calls); short premium in bonds/notes; short premium but long delta in euro; naked short puts in yen. Recently covered corn and soybeans.
- **Natural gas strategy** [48:17]: Prefers call spreads over naked short calls due to upside risk; keeps size small despite large contract. Dislikes UNG (illiquid).

### Liquidity and biotech
- **Liquidity preference** [50:19]: No edge in illiquid underlyings; won't trade them. Market makers can, but not him.
- **Biotech avoidance** [51:29]: Almost never trades biotech due to poor liquidity; only exception is Moderna.

### Earnings and midterms
- **Earnings week** [52:29]: Has positions in Google and Microsoft (trading both); will look at Amazon. Passes on TDOC, BMY, and other lower-liquidity names.
- **Midterm effect** [54:56]: Not currently trading midterm-related plays. Did buy EWZ (Brazil) post-midterm at 30–31, sold at 33 for quick gain; no options, just equity.
- **Extended options hours** [55:47]: Supportive in principle but skeptical it will trade; needs Citadel and other HFT firms to make markets, otherwise no liquidity.
