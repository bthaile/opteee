# Everything You Ever Needed to Know about TIPS Bonds | The Options Trench

## Summary

TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) are US Treasury bonds whose principal adjusts monthly with the Consumer Price Index (CPI-U), locking in a real (inflation-adjusted) return rather than a nominal one. The episode covers TIPS mechanics, pricing, tax implications, and the current market opportunity: real yields of ~2.92% on 10-year TIPS represent a 20-year high, making them attractive relative to both historical standards and current equity valuations. Key risks include fiscal deterioration, potential CPI manipulation, and mark-to-market losses if held before maturity.

## Key takeaways

### Mechanics & structure
- **Principal adjustment**: TIPS principal grows monthly with CPI-U; coupon (fixed %) is applied to the growing principal, so semiannual coupon payments increase over time [02:02–03:12]
- **CPI lag**: The reference CPI used for adjustments is on a ~3-month lag; this lag applies equally at purchase and maturity, so investors are not disadvantaged [17:03]
- **Index ratio**: When buying TIPS, the actual cash outlay = bond price × index ratio (cumulative CPI change since issuance); a bond quoted at $100.72 with 42% cumulative inflation costs ~$143 [19:24]
- **New-issue floor**: Newly issued TIPS have an embedded put option—principal cannot fall below par if deflation occurs; pre-existing TIPS can decline below their purchase index ratio [43:59–45:20]

### Investment framework
- **Real yield vs. nominal yield**: TIPS yield = guaranteed real return above inflation if held to maturity; nominal Treasury yield = bet on inflation [20:39–22:12]
- **Break-even inflation**: The gap between nominal (5.28%) and TIPS (2.92%) yields is the market's implied inflation forecast (~2.36% annually). If you expect inflation > break-even, buy TIPS; if lower, buy nominal bonds [22:12–24:39]
- **Mark-to-market risk**: TIPS prices fall when real yields rise (as happened 2021–2023); losses are real if liquidated early, but the locked real return is yours if held to maturity [04:25–05:40]
- **Hold-to-maturity vs. speculation**: If not holding to maturity, you're speculating on interest rates, not inflation; bonds work best as portfolio diversifiers during deflationary shocks [25:54–32:01]

### Current valuation & opportunity
- **Real yields at 20-year highs**: 10-year TIPS at 2.92% real yield; historical average (2003–present) ~1.0%; current level last seen ~2003 [50:28]
- **Equity comparison**: Ibbotson forecasts 5.6% real equity returns; Elm Wealth (using CAPE yield) implies 2.92% real equity returns—matching TIPS. Stocks are expensive; bonds offer comparable expected return with no volatility [53:24–56:03]
- **Inflation expectations mismatch**: Market prices break-even at 2.36% annually, below recent 3-year average (2.95%) and well below recent 6-month annualized rate (~5%). Market may be discounting a durable disinflation [33:04–35:38]
- **Defensive positioning**: Unlike 2021 (TINA—"there is no alternative"), today bonds offer genuine real yield; a defensive posture is historically justified [56:03–57:27]

### Tax & account structure
- **Phantom income**: Principal gains from CPI adjustment are taxable annually, even though not paid out; hold TIPS only in tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k) [47:52]
- **Liquidity & access**: TIPS available via Treasury Direct, brokers, or ETFs; $2 trillion market (~6% of $32 trillion Treasury market); sufficient depth for most retail investors [03:12, 11:48]

### Risks & caveats
- **Fiscal deterioration**: If US loses creditor confidence, government may suppress rates or tamper with CPI-U methodology to reduce TIPS principal adjustments and Social Security COLAs [01:01:09–01:02:11]
- **Government spending**: Continued fiscal stimulus may justify high equity valuations despite expensive multiples; TIPS returns may lag if private-sector income remains strong [58:35–01:00:01]
- **Valuation sensitivity**: Real yields are mean-reverting; if real yields fall, TIPS prices rise (and vice versa); current 2.92% may not persist [40:19]
