# Why Safe Investors Settle for WORSE Returns (Collar Strategy)

## Summary

A collar strategy caps upside potential in exchange for downside protection, creating an asymmetric payoff that underperforms in normal markets but significantly outperforms during severe drawdowns. The trade-off is intentional: sacrificing annual outperformance to preserve capital and recover faster when systemic shocks occur.

## Key takeaways

- **Collar mechanics as sleep-at-night insurance** [00:00] — The collar's primary purpose is psychological and capital-preservation-focused, not maximum return generation. It trades away upside participation to eliminate downside participation during crashes.

- **Underperformance in normal regimes** [00:00] — Without a major market shock, collars systematically underperform buy-and-hold strategies. This is the explicit cost of the protection.

- **Outperformance during crises** [00:00] — When systemic shocks occur, the collar's dampened drawdown allows faster recovery and cumulative outperformance over multi-year horizons, even if annual returns lag in calm years.

- **Long-term recovery advantage** [00:00] — The time required for buy-and-hold to catch up to a collar's cumulative returns after a crash can be substantial, illustrating the compounding benefit of avoiding large drawdowns.

- **Regime-dependent strategy selection** [00:00] — Collar performance is entirely dependent on whether the market experiences the kind of shock the strategy is designed to hedge. Without it, the strategy underperforms by design.

## Notable quotes

> "You do not need to outperform the market to the upside every year to do well. If you outperform the market during really bad years, notice how long it took buy and hold to catch up."

> "You're sacrificing this upside so that if this happens, you do not participate to the downside anywhere near to the same degree."

## Regime / context

This video presents a regime-dependent analysis of collar strategies. The collar's value proposition is entirely contingent on the occurrence of systemic market shocks; in extended bull markets or low-volatility periods, the strategy will lag buy-and-hold by design. The comparison illustrates the fundamental trade-off between annual return maximization and drawdown minimization—a choice that depends on investor time horizon, risk tolerance, and belief about future market regimes.
