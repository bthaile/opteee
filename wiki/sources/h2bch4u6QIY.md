# Is Low VIX The Best Time to Sell Puts?

## Summary

This analysis examines the relationship between implied volatility levels and the profitability of short put strategies, specifically exploring whether low-VIX environments represent optimal entry points for premium selling. The discussion centers on the variance risk premium and the trade-off between hedge cost and expected returns across different delta selections, with particular attention to the asymmetric risk profile of short strangles.

## Key takeaways

- **Volatility-adjusted efficiency**: Short puts show better risk-adjusted returns at the 13–16 delta range rather than at absolute VIX lows, balancing hedge cost against premium collected [00:00]
- **Variance risk premium mechanics**: Selling 16 delta puts historically generates positive returns in most years, demonstrating the core mechanism of the variance risk premium [00:34]
- **Short strangle fragility**: Short strangles deteriorate rapidly due to asymmetric call-side risk, even when put-side premium appears attractive [00:34]
- **Hedge cost vs. payoff trade-off**: Lower volatility reduces premium but also reduces hedge costs; the optimal zone balances these competing factors rather than simply chasing the lowest VIX [00:00]
