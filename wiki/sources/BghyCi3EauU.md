# When to Use Vertical Spreads (And When Not To) | The Options Trench

## Summary

This episode explores the decision framework for using options—specifically vertical spreads—as surgical trading instruments. Eric and Chris establish two decision gates: (1) do you have a view on timing? and (2) how specific is your price target? They walk through a Coinbase earnings example, comparing outright puts, wide put spreads, and tight put spreads, then apply Kelly criterion sizing to a $5,000 account to determine position size for a 152.5–150 put spread bet.

## Key takeaways

- **Gate 1: Timing view [01:11]** — Options are surgical tools; use them only if you have a view on *when* something will happen. Without timing conviction, buy the stock instead.
- **Gate 2: Price specificity [05:48]** — The tighter your price target, the tighter your vertical spread should be. Wider views → wider spreads → eventually just buying an outright option.
- **Timing and volatility are linked [03:25]** — Having a view on timing automatically gives you a view on volatility. A 10% move in one month implies different vol than a 10% move in one year.
- **Vertical spread payoff structure [17:29]** — A 152.5–150 put spread costs $0.60, pays max $2.50 (3.2:1 return) if stock hits $150 or lower; outright 160 put costs $5, pays $7.50 at $150 (1.5:1 return) but continues to profit on larger drops.
- **Kelly sizing for discrete bets [31:12]** — With 40% win probability and 3.2:1 odds, Kelly prescribes ~20% of bankroll (16 spreads on $5k account); half-Kelly = 8 spreads at $480 cost.
- **Break-even calculation [22:04]** — The 152.5–150 spread breaks even at $151.90; you lose all premium if stock stays above $152.50.
- **Catalyst importance [24:24]** — Earnings creates a discrete, binary outcome window. Without the catalyst, a vertical spread moves slowly and requires active management.
- **Post-trade reality [41:17]** — Coinbase dropped to $137 intraday (>$15 move), far exceeding the $10 straddle. The tight spread still paid max, but an outright put would have captured the excess.
- **Straddle as consensus [44:58]** — Use the market's straddle price as a baseline for expected move size; your edge comes from disagreeing on *shape* (binary vs. smooth distribution), not necessarily size.
- **Return vs. gross profit trade-off [43:39]** — High percent returns (3.2:1) on tight spreads come with narrow win zones; wider spreads or outright options offer lower percent returns but capture tail moves.

## Notable quotes
