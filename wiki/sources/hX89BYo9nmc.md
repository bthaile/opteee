# Why Outperforming SPY Isn't Enough When Running The Wheel

## Summary

Running the wheel strategy requires outperforming SPY by more than a simple percentage margin because each trade cycle incurs friction costs (commissions, slippage, bid-ask spreads) and tax consequences. A naive 1% annual outperformance over 10 years becomes drastically negative after accounting for these real-world costs, making the hurdle rate for wheel profitability substantially higher than passive index returns.

## Key takeaways

- **Friction compounds across cycles** [00:00] — Each interaction with the market (selling puts, assignment, selling calls, buyback) carries transaction costs; these accumulate across the many occurrences inherent in the wheel strategy.
- **Tax drag is material** [00:00] — Short-term capital gains, assignment events, and frequent realization of gains create tax liabilities that passive buy-and-hold strategies avoid.
- **Naive outperformance math fails** [00:00] — A 1% annual outperformance target over 10 years sounds reasonable until friction and taxes are included; the strategy then underperforms significantly.
- **Hurdle rate must be higher** [00:00] — To justify the wheel's active trading and tax burden, the gross return must exceed SPY by a meaningful margin (not just 1%) to break even after costs.

## Notable quotes

> "You cannot look at it as, 'Oh, I just need to outperform the spy by a percent over a 10-year window.' Because once you include costs and all that stuff, you're drastically underperforming."

## Regime / context

This is a foundational principle for any active options strategy: the hurdle rate for outperformance must account for all real costs. The wheel, despite its mechanical appeal, is not exempt from this reality. Traders often underestimate the cumulative drag of commissions, bid-ask spreads, and tax events when comparing to passive alternatives.
