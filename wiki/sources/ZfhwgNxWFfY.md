# Is the Wheel Strategy Actually Worth It?

## Summary

The wheel strategy—selling cash-secured puts, taking assignment, then selling covered calls—is often marketed as a way to "get paid twice." However, the strategy has structural limitations: assignment conditions may change unfavorably, and covered calls cap upside profit potential. A disciplined trader must weigh these trade-offs objectively rather than accepting the strategy's popular narrative uncritically.

## Key takeaways

- **Premium collection vs. assignment risk** [00:00] Selling cash-secured puts collects premium upfront, but conditions that made assignment acceptable may shift before expiration, creating regret or forced holding at unfavorable prices.
- **Capped upside on the call leg** [00:00] Selling covered calls generates additional premium but sacrifices unlimited upside; the trader misses significant profit if the stock rallies past the strike.
- **Objective strategy evaluation** [00:00] Rather than accepting marketing narratives ("get paid twice"), traders should list both benefits and drawbacks, then decide whether the trade-offs align with their goals and risk tolerance.

## Notable quotes

> "The problem with that, as a lot of you know, is conditions change. The point that you might have been happy taking assignment might change in the future depending on what's going on in the world."
