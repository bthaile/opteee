# You Think Price Goes Up. Now What?

## Summary

A directional view—"price goes up"—is only one of four location claims available to traders, but it's the most common entry point. Rather than immediately selecting from the full universe of options structures, traders should use filtering questions to naturally narrow the decision space and identify which structure actually fits their thesis and constraints.

## Key takeaways

- **Directional bias is a starting point, not a complete thesis** [00:00]. Believing price will rise eliminates roughly half of available structures, but doesn't determine which remaining structure is optimal.
- **Structure selection requires filtering, not immediate choice** [00:00]. The process is not "I think it goes up → pick the best structure from all options," but rather a series of filtering questions that naturally converge on the right tool.
- **Location claims are one of four dimensions** [00:00]. Directional hypothesis is necessary but insufficient for structure design; other constraints (risk tolerance, capital, time frame, Greeks management) drive the final decision.
