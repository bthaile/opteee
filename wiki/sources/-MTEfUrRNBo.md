# The Drawdown Rule That Saves Traders from Blowing Up

## Summary

Portfolio-level drawdown management requires defining an account-size-based risk budget and establishing circuit breakers that trigger automatic position cuts when breached. The framework assumes that larger expected returns necessitate larger potential drawdowns, and that hitting a pre-defined drawdown threshold signals an unaccounted-for market regime change requiring immediate de-risking.

## Key takeaways

- **Define drawdown budget at portfolio level** [00:00] — Start by establishing your account size and total downside tolerance; this is your risk budget and reflects your true risk tolerance.
- **Larger returns require larger drawdowns** [00:00] — Traders seeking outsized upside must accept correspondingly larger potential drawdowns; the inverse relationship is unavoidable.
- **Use circuit breakers as automatic triggers** [00:00] — Monitor drawdown thresholds and implement mechanical position cuts when breached; these act as kill switches for uncontemplated risk.
- **Drawdown breach signals regime change** [00:00] — If your pre-defined drawdown is hit, it means something unexpected has occurred that you haven't modeled; this is the signal to stop and reassess.
- **Cut positions immediately upon breach** [00:00] — Once a circuit breaker is triggered, begin reducing exposure without delay; the breach itself is evidence that your thesis or risk model has failed.
