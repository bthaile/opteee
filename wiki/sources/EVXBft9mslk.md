### Evergreen mechanics

- **Delta-neutral long straddle P&L breakdown** [12:56–24:17]: With +10 gamma per $1 move (per option, ×100 multiplier), four consecutive $2 moves yield $80 gross gamma profit; daily theta cost of $60 leaves net $20 P&L. Gamma scalping profit comes from rehedging to delta-neutral after each move, not from directional conviction.

- **Gamma and delta interaction** [15:35–20:22]: In a long straddle, both long call and long put start ~50 delta. As underlying rises, call gains delta and put loses delta → net long deltas. Inverse on downside. Gamma measures the rate of delta change per $1 move. Surrogate options technique: simulate price moves by looking at adjacent strikes in the chain to visualize delta/gamma behavior without recalculating.

- **Long premium vs. short premium time-frame mismatch** [41:53–44:15]: Long premium (debit spreads, long calls/puts) benefits from time decay less acutely, so longer expirations are reasonable. Short premium (credit spreads, short calls/puts) suffers from time decay over long windows; selling 66 days out on a short call is unfavorable. CG's IBIT position exemplified this mistake.

- **Vertical spread risk-reward trade-off** [30:26–32:03]: Verticals typically show lower reward-to-risk ratios (e.g., risk $800–$1,000 to make $200–$300) compared to directional single options. This is normal and balanced by higher probability of profit; position sizing compensates.

- **Position management and stops on verticals** [36:30–37:17]: Placing hard stops on verticals defeats the purpose—you're trading the distribution within the spread, not a binary outcome. Stops should be structured carefully to preserve the spread's intended payoff profile.

- **Earnings premium and expiration selection** [45:34–46:25]: Earnings-driven IV expansion affects expirations *containing* the earnings date. Pre-earnings expirations see no boost; post-earnings expirations capture the premium. Weeklies appear ~30–40 days out depending on underlying liquidity (SPY has weeklies out to ~49 days).

- **Merger/acquisition optionality** [47:03–50:45]: When a deal is announced but not finalized, options retain significant value because deal terms can shift or fall through. As the deal solidifies and a known delisting date approaches, liquidity evaporates and positions become illiquid. Twitter is a recent example of this behavior.

- **Hard edges vs. soft edges** [57:01–59:52]: Hard edges are structural, repeatable, and consistent (e.g., forced flow, beta premium). Soft edges are discretionary and contextual (e.g., stock picking, regime calls). Retail traders have asymmetric opportunity in soft edges when skill is applied, but hard edges form a reliable base layer. Both are valuable; research and backtesting are essential.

- **Learning habit formation** [53:35–56:32]: Overcome procrastination by chunking learning into 2-minute sessions rather than full videos. Pair with daily AI-generated single-question quizzes (like Duolingo) to reinforce terminology and concepts incrementally.
