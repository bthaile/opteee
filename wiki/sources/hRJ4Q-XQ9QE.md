# Structural Risk vs Managed Risk: How to Trade Bigger

## Summary

The core principle is distinguishing between **structural risk** (the maximum loss if a position is left unmanaged to expiration or assignment) and **managed risk** (the expected loss when active management is applied). By accurately modeling the managed-risk outcome, traders can size into larger positions for the same total capital at risk, provided the management plan is sound and aligned with their strategy.

## Key takeaways

- **Structural vs. managed risk framework** [00:00]: Structural risk is the full loss if a trade goes unmanaged; managed risk accounts for active adjustments and exit discipline. The difference allows larger position sizing.
- **Position-sizing math** [00:00]: If each contract carries ~$1,820 structural risk and you have $5,800 to risk, you can trade 3 contracts at structural risk. If management reduces average loss to ~$480 per contract, the same $5,800 budget now supports a larger position.
- **Management must be intentional** [00:00]: Larger sizing is only justified if you have a documented, strategy-aligned management plan. Do not use managed-risk math as an excuse to over-leverage without a real adjustment protocol.
- **Risk budget allocation** [00:00]: Once you know your managed-risk per contract, you can allocate your total risk budget more efficiently across multiple positions or strategies.

## Notable quotes

> "If I know I'm going to manage these on average at say 480 per contract, that means for that same risk, because there's a management element to it, I can trade a bigger size."
