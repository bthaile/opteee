# The Truth About Value Investing: Why Most Investors Get It Wrong

## Summary

This deep-dive analysis examines whether value investing—the strategy of buying cheap stocks—still works after a century of market data. The presenter tests eight major claims about value investing and finds that while the factor historically outperformed for 80 years (1926–2006), it has underperformed dramatically since 2007, with a drawdown of 58% from peak and currently trading at only 14× the multiple of growth stocks versus the historical 25×. The research concludes that the textbook version of value investing appears broken, though a smaller ~5% annual premium may persist, and that combining value with momentum or using options-based hedging offers conditional improvements.

## Key takeaways

- **Value's historical dominance [01:05]**: From 1926 to 2006, cheap stocks beat expensive ones by 25×. Peak was February 2007; current gap is down 43% from that peak (14× vs. 25×).

- **Definition and measurement [02:14]**: Value stocks trade at ~1.2× book value; growth stocks at ~12×. Common metrics include price-to-book, P/E ratio, price-to-cash-flow, and dividend-to-price. All four metrics moved together in 2007, suggesting a structural shift, not a measurement problem.

- **Monthly returns have flipped negative [04:33]**: Pre-2007, value returned +0.43% to +0.50% monthly consistently. Post-2007, value returns −0.1% monthly. Value lost money in 42% of years even before the 2007 slump.

- **Claim 1 holds: cheap beats expensive over a century [05:00]**: Objectively true across 100 years, but the recent reversal is unprecedented and statistically significant (t-stat −2.09, clearing 95% confidence threshold).

- **Effect is stronger in small caps but exists in large caps [06:05]**: Small-cap value shows stronger effect, but large-cap value also exhibits the factor pre-2007, just smaller in magnitude.

- **Post-2007 drawdown is unprecedented [07:25]**: 57.8% drawdown is the deepest in value's entire lifespan. 8+ years from peak to bottom—longer than any prior slump. Simulations show this is 2.3% likely under old rules (extremely rare).

- **Interest rates barely affect value returns [10:13]**: Direction of rates has minimal impact on monthly value returns (low t-stat, likely due to chance).

- **Book value is not uniquely broken [11:00]**: All four valuation metrics (P/B, P/E, cash-flow, dividends) deteriorated simultaneously in 2007, ruling out measurement error.

- **Crowding did not kill value [11:33]**: After Fama–French published their 1992 paper, value returned +0.42% monthly (vs. +0.55% before), contradicting the "crowding kills anomalies" hypothesis. McLean & Pontiff (2016) found published anomalies typically fall 58% post-publication; value went up.

- **Value protects in selective crashes [13:07]**: Value beat the market in 6 out of 10 major crashes (>20% drawdowns). Protection occurs when expensive stocks deflate, not during broad panics. 2008, 2020 saw everything fall together.

- **Quality filters show weak statistical edge [14:40]**: Cheap + profitable stocks earned 16.9% vs. 15.8% for all cheap stocks (t-stat 0.77, not significant). Within cheapest quintile, profitable half earned 9.2% vs. 6.5% for unprofitable half (t-stat 1.13, not significant). Value traps are real but hard to filter statistically.

- **Value timing is weak but directional [16:07]**: Value is cheaper than growth in 87% of years since 1926; currently at 87th percentile (unusually cheap). Spread-based timing forecast explains only 6% of year-to-year swings (t-stat 1.6). June 2000 drove most of the 5.1% outperformance over 36 years.

- **Covered calls cap upside without adequate downside protection [18:53]**: Selling calls at mid-strike yields ~6.5% income but sacrifices significant upside. Worst drawdown reduced from ~60% to ~48%—reasonable but expensive trade-off.

- **Short puts offer lower volatility but worse risk-adjusted returns [18:53]**: As underlying moves in your favor, you lose delta and participate less. Lower volatility but not compensated by risk premium; still caps upside.

- **Owning value outright beats option strategies [19:00]**: At equivalent risk, owning the value ETF outright outperformed covered calls, protective puts, collars, and short puts.

- **Hedged value reduces crash losses [20:09]**: 10-delta puts and collars both reduced losses in post-2007 crashes. Collar structure performed well, especially in large moves (2020 timing benefit from monthly put spanning the crash).

- **Value premium is not richer vs. growth or S&P [20:30]**: Contrary to mean-reversion intuition, value premium is not currently attractive relative to growth or broad market.

- **Value + momentum is a powerful combination [21:34]**: Pairing value with momentum factor yields stronger results than value alone. Addresses the "waiting" problem inherent in pure value.

- **Current state: textbook value appears gone [21:34]**: The classical value premium (~15% annual) has largely disappeared. A smaller ~5% premium may persist, but it is regime-dependent and unreliable. As of 2026, value is up 61% from 2020 lows but still 32% below the 2006 peak.

## Regime / context

**Date:** November 2026. This analysis spans 100 years of US equity data (1926–2026) with particular focus on the structural break in 2007 and the ongoing underperformance of value through 2026. The presenter emphasizes that value investing's historical edge has deteriorated significantly and may not recover to textbook levels. The research is pre-registered (claims ordered 1, 6, 2, 8 rather than sequentially, per the presenter's note on methodology). Confidence in the statistical findings is high for the long-term historical comparison but medium for forward-looking predictions, given regime uncertainty.
