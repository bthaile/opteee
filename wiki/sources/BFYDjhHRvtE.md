# Is Hedging SPY Worth It?

## Summary

A historical backtest comparing unhedged [[spy]] holdings against persistent hedging strategies using out-of-the-money and at-the-money protective puts from 2007 onward. The analysis reveals that while hedges provide significant protection during early drawdowns (e.g., the 2008 financial crisis), accumulated profits and the perpetual drag of premium costs erode the hedge's net benefit over longer horizons, particularly during recovery phases like the COVID bear market.

## Key takeaways

- **Hedge effectiveness is path-dependent** [00:00–00:31]: Hedges provide outsized protection early in a portfolio's life when unrealized losses dominate; once profits accumulate, the same hedge becomes a drag.
- **10% OTM vs. ATM trade-off** [00:00–00:31]: Both strategies tested; ATM hedges show consistent cost drag, while 10% OTM offers a middle ground but still underperforms unhedged during recovery.
- **2008 GFC protection** [00:00–00:31]: Hedged positions significantly outperformed during the global financial crisis because the portfolio had minimal accumulated P&L to offset hedge costs.
- **COVID bear market underperformance** [00:31]: Even 10% OTM hedges lagged unhedged [[spy]] during the 2020 drawdown, as accumulated gains made the perpetual premium cost a net drag.
- **Perpetual drag is the core issue** [00:31]: The cost of continuously rolling protective puts acts as a structural headwind that compounds over time, especially in bull markets.

## Regime / context

This backtest spans 2007–present, capturing multiple market regimes: the 2008 global financial crisis (where hedges shined), the post-crisis bull market (where hedges dragged), the COVID-19 bear market (2020), and subsequent recovery. The analysis highlights a critical tension in portfolio insurance: early protection is valuable when capital is at risk, but the perpetual cost of rolling hedges becomes a drag once the portfolio has generated substantial unrealized gains. This regime-dependent payoff structure suggests that static hedging strategies may be suboptimal compared to dynamic or conditional hedging approaches.
