# Options Collar Strategy Explained: Payoff Diagram and Ratios

## Summary

A collar strategy combines a long put (downside protection) with a short call (upside cap) to create a defined-risk position. The strategy is designed to reduce the cost of downside protection by selling call premium to finance the long put, resulting in a capped payoff profile on both sides. At a 1:1 ratio, the structure is symmetric: one long put and one short call per 100 shares held.

## Key takeaways

- **Core motivation**: Start with a long put to cap downside, then sell a call to finance it [00:00]
- **1:1 ratio structure**: For every 100 shares, hold one long put and sell one short call to achieve the classic collar payoff [00:00]
- **Payoff shape**: Creates a defined-risk, defined-profit zone with both downside and upside boundaries [00:00]
- **Ratio flexibility**: The strategy can be tweaked by selling fewer calls, allowing for asymmetric adjustments to the payoff profile [00:00]
