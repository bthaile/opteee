# Building Options Trades | Drive-Thru Trading

## Summary

Eric and CG explore options trading fundamentals through live market analysis and trade construction. Starting with implied volatility mechanics and the volatility surface, they walk through how to identify trade ideas using technical analysis and macro themes, then build two concrete options positions (a long call on KTOS and a short call spread on ROST) within a sample $25k portfolio. The session emphasizes process-based decision-making, objective analysis, and the importance of tracking trades and themes over time.

## Key takeaways

- **Implied volatility is the unknown input** [32:50]: Of the seven inputs to an option pricing model (spot, type, strike, DTE, rate, yield, IV), only IV is unknown and forward-looking. It's the only variable that must be forecast; the rest are knowns.

- **Out-of-the-money options are used for volatility measurement** [17:22]: When measuring IV, skew, or term structure, use OTM options, not ITM, because ITM options contain intrinsic value that creates noise in the volatility signal.

- **Volatility surface reveals market structure** [41:58]: The IV surface (combining skew and term structure) shows how volatility varies across strikes and expirations. Valleys or anomalies in the surface can signal mispricing or illiquidity, but true arbitrage is rare due to HFT.

- **Sticky strike vs. sticky delta** [41:16]: When comparing options across expirations, sticky strike holds the strike constant; sticky delta holds the delta constant. The choice affects how you model price moves and volatility changes.

- **Event volatility can be isolated mathematically** [14:52]: If an earnings event falls within an expiration, you can strip out the event vol from the term vol using a formula, leaving the residual "normal day" volatility.

- **Contrarian identity constrains opportunity** [01:09:00]: Adopting "contrarian trader" as an identity causes the brain to reject valid setups (e.g., breakouts after gaps). Instead, use specific contrarian *strategies* for reversals while remaining flexible on directional approach.

- **Process beats style; money beats identity** [01:11:21]: Don't marry yourself to asset classes, ideologies, or time frames. The job is to make money; whatever gets you there is the right tool.

- **Technical analysis requires objective backing** [01:05:17]: Patterns like descending wedges should be validated against historical data (e.g., "How often do descending wedges in GLD lead to continuation?") rather than relied on as intuition alone.

- **Macro themes must be tracked and dated** [01:38:43]: Record your active macro themes (e.g., "long defense," "short AI") with entry and exit dates so you can later analyze how many trades occurred per theme and how long each theme remained valid.

- **Vertical spreads have minimal Greek footprint** [02:14:57]: Narrow vertical spreads (e.g., 280/290 call spread) have offsetting Greeks; delta, gamma, theta, and vega are all small. You're trading distribution, not Greeks. Wider spreads act more like naked options and have higher Greek sensitivity.

- **Credit spread = short vertical; debit spread = long vertical** [02:29:28]: A spread is a credit spread if you sell the closer-to-money leg (net credit received); it's a debit spread if you buy the closer-to-money leg (net debit paid). The directional preference flips accordingly.

- **Long calls for strong directional conviction; verticals for level-set views** [02:08:15]: If you expect a large move, buy the put/call (asymmetric gamma). If you just think the underlying won't exceed a level, sell a vertical (narrow Greeks, distribution play).

- **Broker platform matters for options analysis** [51:14]: Robinhood lacks the data tools needed for serious options work. IBKR (Trader Workstation) or Schwab (thinkorswim) provide payoff diagrams, Greeks, and portfolio-level analysis essential for learning.

- **Portfolio payoff diagram shows net exposure** [53:58]: thinkorswim allows you to view your entire portfolio as a single payoff diagram, revealing net delta, gamma, and vega exposure across all positions at once.

- **Trade log with macro themes enables data analysis** [01:42:01]: Structure your trade log with separate columns for theme, direction, and date so you can later query how many trades per theme, average holding period, and theme performance.
