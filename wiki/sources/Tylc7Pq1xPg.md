# META Earnings, FOMC, and a New Trader | Drive-Thru Trading

## Summary

Eric introduces a new segment called "Drive-Thru Trading" featuring Stephen, a market analyst new to derivatives trading. The episode covers volatility term structure mechanics through a detailed question-of-the-day exercise, explores recent market dynamics around tech sector pullbacks and capex concerns, and culminates in building Stephen's first paper-trading position: a short put spread on Amazon ahead of earnings. The core educational focus is understanding how volatility behaves differently across expiration terms and why mean-reverting assets like VIX require nuanced position management.

## Key takeaways

### Dated market read (2026-07-30)

- **Tech sector pullback without clear catalyst** [43:02]: Momentum stocks (semiconductors, AI infrastructure, cloud) have pulled back aggressively despite continued capex increases from Mag 7 companies; market appears to be front-running expectations of capex contraction.
- **Semiconductor valuation disconnect** [45:07]: Micron trading at single-digit forward PE (up 564% YoY), Nvidia at ~18x forward PE; market skepticism suggests belief that capex numbers will decline significantly over next 12 months.
- **Amazon pre-earnings positioning** [57:14]: Amazon underperforming SPY on relative strength, generally net short premium, larger positions further out in time; historically behaves well into earnings but without outsized returns.

### Evergreen mechanics

- **Volatility term structure and mean reversion** [09:37]–[27:45]: VIX is a mean-reverting asset (5-year range ~17–25); front-month volatility moves dramatically while longer-dated volatility is more stable. Market prices in reversion to mean, so front-month spikes don't propagate uniformly across the curve.
- **Contango vs. backwardation shift** [14:14]–[15:07]: Day-over-day shift from backwardation (VIX 18.21, Aug 19) to contango (VIX 20.66, Aug 20.31) signals structural change in market expectations; curve typically upward-sloping because uncertainty increases further out.
- **Vega sensitivity across terms** [31:05]–[37:03]: Vega (sensitivity to IV change) is higher for longer-dated options because the underlying term's IV moves less dramatically. A 23-day option with vega 0.22 vs. 79-day option with vega 0.40 reflects different volatility regimes, not option sensitivity per se.
- **Why spreads can lose despite rising volatility** [20:09]–[30:23]: In a short Aug / long Oct VIX futures spread, if front-month volatility spikes but back-month rises less (due to mean reversion), the spread loses despite Oct vol rising. Rate of change is asymmetric across the curve.
- **Put skew in indices vs. call skew in individual stocks** [38:07]–[38:56]: S&P 500 exhibits put skew (downside protection demand); GameStop exhibits call skew (uncertainty around Roaring Kitty catalyst). Skew reflects market's asymmetric risk perception.
- **Vertical spreads as directional bets** [01:05:21]–[01:06:38]: Long debit spread (buy 230 call, sell 235 call) requires move above 230 for max profit (~48% probability); short put spread (sell 210 put, buy 205 put) requires stay above 210 (~60% probability). Trade-off: lower probability of max profit vs. higher probability of profit.
- **Leverage in spreads vs. single shares** [01:20:11]: A 2-lot short put spread on Amazon with 15% underlying move yields ~$87.50 profit vs. $34 on one share; leverage is real but capped by spread width.
- **Exit decision framework** [01:20:57]–[01:22:35]: Assess remaining profit potential vs. capital tied up; if position has captured 80% of max profit in 3 weeks of 15-week expiration, consider whether $40 remaining is worth the capital. Can also roll strikes higher to extend upside capture without closing.
