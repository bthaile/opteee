# Avoiding Value Traps with Quality Filters

## Summary

Quality filters—specifically profitability screens—can meaningfully reduce exposure to value traps. Analysis comparing cheap-and-profitable stocks versus cheap-and-unprofitable stocks shows quality value outperforming plain value by ~110 basis points annually, with a t-statistic of 0.77. While not conventionally statistically significant, the effect is persistent and economically meaningful, though quality value exhibits lower Sharpe ratios and deeper drawdowns than plain value strategies.

## Key takeaways

- **Value trap identification [00:00]**: Cheap and unprofitable stocks represent the weakest performance cohort; they are the core of value trap risk.
- **Quality value outperformance [00:00]**: Cheap-and-profitable stocks earned 16.9% annually versus 15.8% for all cheap stocks—a 110 bps spread.
- **Statistical caveat [00:00]**: The t-statistic of 0.77 falls below conventional significance thresholds (1.96), but the effect is persistent enough to warrant attention.
- **Risk-adjusted trade-off [00:00]**: Quality value strategies show lower Sharpe ratios and deeper maximum drawdowns than plain value, indicating higher volatility or tail risk despite higher returns.
- **Practical filter implication [00:00]**: Profitability as a quality screen reduces but does not eliminate value trap exposure; it is one layer of protection, not a complete solution.

## Regime / context

This analysis reflects historical backtested performance and does not account for regime shifts, market structure changes, or forward-looking factor crowding. The modest statistical significance (t = 0.77) suggests the quality-value edge is real but fragile; practitioners should combine profitability screens with additional filters (e.g., balance-sheet strength, cash flow stability) to reduce false positives.
