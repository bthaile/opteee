# Why the Wheel Strategy Fails on SPY

## Summary

The wheel strategy—a mechanical sequence of cash-secured puts followed by covered calls—is not a coherent strategy but a series of disconnected trades. When applied to SPY, it fundamentally misaligns with the underlying's upward drift: the initial short-put leg caps upside profit while retaining unlimited downside risk, creating an asymmetric payoff profile that works against the market's structural tendency to rise.

## Key takeaways

- **The wheel is not a strategy; it's a trade sequence** [00:00]. It lacks a unified thesis and instead chains together individual positions.
- **Short puts cap upside while retaining unlimited downside** [00:00]. This asymmetry is particularly problematic in an asset with positive drift like SPY.
- **Structural mismatch with SPY's upward bias** [00:00]. SPY tends to appreciate over time, yet the wheel's initial leg (short put) does not participate in that upside beyond the premium collected.
- **Risk-reward inversion** [00:00]. The position structure violates basic asymmetric-return principles: you accept large downside for small, capped upside.
