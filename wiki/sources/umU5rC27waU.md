# Understanding Structural vs Managed Risk

## Summary

This video distinguishes between two critical dimensions of risk control in options trading: **structural risk** (the maximum capital at stake in a trade setup) and **managed risk** (the actual loss threshold a trader intends to enforce through active management). The key insight is that capital allocation is the only fully controllable variable for an options trader, and understanding this distinction clarifies how to size positions and set exit rules.

## Key takeaways

- **Structural risk is the cost of entry** [00:00–00:15]: The structural risk equals the full capital outlay required to establish a position. For a long put purchased at $1, structural risk is $1.
- **Managed risk is the intended loss threshold** [00:15–00:30]: Managed risk reflects the trader's plan to exit or adjust *before* realizing the full structural loss. It is the loss level the trader intends to enforce through active management.
- **Capital allocation is the only fully controllable lever** [00:00–00:05]: A trader cannot control market direction or volatility, but can always control how much capital to deploy.
- **Defined-risk vs. undefined-risk strategies differ in complexity** [00:40–00:50]: Some strategies (e.g., spreads) have defined risk built into the structure; others (e.g., naked options, long single options) require active management to cap losses.
