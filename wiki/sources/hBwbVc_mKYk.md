# Tom Sosnoff on Moderna, Nvidia, and Bonds | Unhedged

## Summary

Tom Sosnoff discusses the extraordinary move in Moderna (roughly doubling from ~60 to ~160), analyzing the options chain dynamics, market-maker positioning, and gamma risk embedded in the move. He contrasts this with semiconductor plays (Intel long, short premium in Micron/AMD/SMH), addresses Nvidia's narrow expected move ahead of earnings, and pivots to macro themes: bond market intervention, geopolitical posturing, and the thesis that capitalism has swallowed democracy in driving market outcomes.

## Key takeaways

### Dated market read (2026-08-25)

- **Moderna's extraordinary move** [00:53–02:53]: Stock up ~100+ points in one day; market makers initially devastated by ratio-spread losses (e.g., selling 90 calls at 50¢ delta, then stock moves $100, losing $55k on a $50 intended profit). Now 100 vol in OTM calls; only ~5% probability of another $100 move. Strikes capped at 260 (likely exchange bandwidth/quote limitations); November expiration goes to 360.
- **Volatility term structure** [07:07–08:15]: Front-month vol at 100–95; unlikely to drop below 80 in next quarters due to undefined upside risk. Puts stay bid because call-side and put-side vol move together; asymmetric upside risk keeps both elevated.
- **Timing and contrarian entry** [09:20–11:08]: Tom avoided shorting day one; waited day two or three. Day two opened ~149, settled lower; markets still $1–2 wide, so he sold a few strangles intraday but exited (bid-ask too wide for his taste).
- **Nvidia earnings tomorrow** [39:26–44:05]: Expected move only $11 (~4.5%); too narrow for Tom to sell premium. Historically well-behaved; hasn't moved outside expected range since May 2024. Prefers selling Q premium (31% IV) as a surrogate earnings play instead; considering Friday or October expiration.
- **Semiconductor positioning** [39:26–40:35]: Long delta in Intel (bought yesterday at ~85); short premium (strangles) in Micron, AMD, SMH; no directional risk in those. Biggest equity risk currently in bonds and S&P/NASDAQ.
- **Bond market intervention** [50:01–51:08]: Treasury moved $2–4B to support bonds; market rallied one day, then sold back to new lows. Bond market is $30T; Fed/Treasury moves are negligible relative to market size. Nvidia alone has $313B implied move tomorrow. Intervention is "PR" and "marketing," not market-moving.
- **Capital usage and position count** [45:29]: 72 total positions; capital usage 25–30% (low for Tom, usually 30–60%). Biggest risk in bond market, then S&P/NASDAQ; minimal equity risk.

### Evergreen mechanics

- **Market-maker bandwidth and strike limitations** [03:54–05:04]: Lead market makers (mostly Citadel) set base volatilities; other HFT firms tweak around them. Bandwidth constraints limit quotes per second and characters on screens. Strike expansion takes days to weeks; used to take longer, now often overnight.
- **Pit trading legacy and position reconciliation** [21:28–27:13]: Back-office reconciliation was manual; Tom could recall exact P&L and contract count to the penny on 99% of days. Daily "vig" (costs) were 40–50% of gross profit. Errors happened daily (cost of business); brokers' errors fell on traders.
- **Liquidity and order size** [13:14–17:16]: Tom maintains discipline: same position size across liquid and illiquid underlyings (e.g., same notional in Nvidia as Moderna). Larger orders attract more market makers (scale effect); no additional slippage on 500-lot vs. 5-lot because market makers are paid by edge × volume, not by direction.
- **Volatility as price, not news** [56:38–57:54]: Catalysts are price-driven, not geopolitical or macro. Gold rallied because it got cheap; Bitcoin rallied because it got cheap; bonds rallied because they got cheap. Stocks expensive → sell off. No macro narrative needed.
- **Capitalism vs. democracy** [53:04–55:23]: US capital markets (stock + bonds + derivatives) are 10–20× the size of all global GDPs combined. Companies like Nvidia are too big to fail. Political/Fed statements are irrelevant relative to market size. Capitalism has swallowed democracy as the primary driver of outcomes.

## Notable quotes

- "What did they do, cure cancer?" [01:44] — on Moderna's move, echoing the absurdity of massive single-day rallies in large-cap biotech.
- "I'm not selling this for another day or two. I'm not touching this from the short side for another day or two." [09:20] — Tom's immediate reaction to the gap-up; discipline to wait for mean-reversion setup.
- "The market goes to size. It couldn't care less what the trade is." [17:16] — on why larger orders attract more liquidity and better fills.
