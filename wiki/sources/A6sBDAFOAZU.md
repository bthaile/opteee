# cg tries to trade | Drive-Thru Trading

## Summary

Eric and CG discuss portfolio concentration risk, position sizing discipline, and the mental hurdles of trading a small account. CG reviews his current positions (all long calls/spreads, ~15% portfolio risk across five trades) and his sector-rotation strategy research, which involved backtesting multiple moving-average configurations over 25 years. Eric emphasizes that sustainable trading requires managing drawdown risk and avoiding oversizing, even on high-conviction plays.

## Key takeaways

### Dated market read (2026-09-10)

- **Market concentration vs. breadth divergence** [08:22–09:45]: SPY near all-time highs (+2.2%) while equal-weight RSP down ~4%, indicating outsized influence from a small handful of mega-cap stocks; energy and healthcare are the only sectors positive over the past month; Russell 2000 (IWM) down ~5%, suggesting small-cap weakness and potential rate-sensitivity concerns.
- **Sector performance snapshot** [11:54–12:30]: Energy and healthcare positive; insurance down 4.7% (worst performer); capital markets within financials up; consumer finance and commercial banks under pressure.

### Evergreen mechanics

- **Concentration risk assessment** [18:27–21:48]: Five positions each representing ~2% account risk, all directional (long calls/spreads), totaling >10% portfolio risk. This creates vulnerability to a 2–3 day market downturn wiping out ~20% of account. Concentration should be the exception, not the rule.
- **Position sizing via spread width** [43:53–44:18]: Define risk by spread width (e.g., $5 wide = passive management; wider spreads require active management). Narrower spreads allow longer holding periods without oversizing.
- **Delayed gratification and compounding** [25:30–27:30]: Small accounts don't generate wealth directly; the value is learning sustainable process and compounding over time. Most people fail because they prioritize short-term comfort over long-term edge development.
- **Asymmetric value creation** [31:03–32:07]: Time-for-money exchange is a false constraint; understanding that value is context-dependent (e.g., a surgeon's $35k for <1 hour vs. $40–50/hr wage earner) unlocks asymmetric upside through leverage, skill, or market inefficiency.
- **Backtesting pitfalls** [48:24–53:47]: Testing multiple configurations over 25 years can obscure period-specific outperformance (e.g., 70% of returns from 2008–2012). Segment results by year to identify trend robustness; use forward simulation (Monte Carlo) to assess which variant is likely to outperform next 5–10 years, not just historical total return.
- **Strategy outline discipline** [54:07–55:45]: A strategy outline requires specific format (not just rule set); includes management rules, RFI (military term for "request for information" / reconnaissance), and clear decision criteria. Paper trading and forward simulations precede live execution.
