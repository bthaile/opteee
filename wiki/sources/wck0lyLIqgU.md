# The Cost of Hedging in Trading

## Summary

Hedging reduces risk but carries a real cost—not just in premium paid, but in opportunity forgone. A collar structure (long put + short call) illustrates the fundamental trade-off: buying downside protection (puts) is expensive, especially in indices, so traders often sell calls against puts to finance or offset that cost. The lesson is that risk reduction and upside capture are in tension.

## Key takeaways

- **Hedging is not free** [00:00] — reducing risk requires accepting a cost, whether explicit (premium paid) or implicit (capped upside).
- **Puts are expensive** [00:00] — protective puts, especially on indices, carry significant premium that must be paid upfront.
- **Collar mechanics** [00:00] — selling a call against a long put can finance part or all of the put cost, but caps your upside at the short call strike.
- **Trade-off acceptance** [00:00] — hedging is an accepted trade-off; the goal is to understand and price the cost, not eliminate it.
