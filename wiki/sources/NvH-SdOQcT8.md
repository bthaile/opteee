# Market Internals & 40K Milestone | Outlier Pro

## Summary

Eric celebrates hitting 40,000 YouTube subscribers and reflects on channel growth and content strategy evolution. The bulk of the session focuses on FOMC preparation: analyzing market internals (breadth, volatility term structure, and price action) across historical rate-hike, rate-cut, and hold scenarios. He presents data-driven frameworks for trading the event and its aftermath, emphasizing that predicting the outcome is less important than understanding how markets typically respond to each scenario.

## Key takeaways

### Dated market read (2026-09-16)

- **40K milestone & content strategy** [13:43–32:06]: Channel has grown steadily month-over-month; 100K is the ultimate target (YouTube play button threshold). Eric is developing a sample portfolio concept to bridge the gap between beginner-friendly education and intermediate-level trading, avoiding trade-alert services while providing end-to-end examples. Upcoming co-hosted workshop with Chris from Robot Wealth to expose viewers to different operational approaches.

- **Market internals deteriorating into FOMC** [32:28–36:46]: Composite Breadth Profile (CBP) is "not good" and approaching dangerous levels. Volatility (VIX) remains subtle. Breadth has flipped negative on advance/decline. Market drifting downward into FOMC rather than sideways (atypical). Small caps (Russell 2000) have rolled off ~7% from highs, suggesting market is pricing in a rate hike.

- **Rate hike probability & geopolitical context** [38:45–44:57]: Fed Watch tool shows ~92% odds of a hike; small-cap weakness supports this. However, Iran war flaring up, pushing oil/gas prices higher and raising inflation persistence risk. Trump administration likely opposes a hike heading into midterms. Eric's base case: no hike, but market is already repricing downside; if a hike occurs, it would be "really rough" for Republicans.

### Evergreen mechanics

- **FOMC historical patterns (hike scenario)** [47:14–50:41]: Coming into a hike, IWM often sells off; SPY shows muted movement that expands over the following week (broadly negative), then reverses over the month. This is "absolutely tradable." Following week is volatile and trends downside; following month shows strongest reversal in hold environments. Small caps disproportionately impacted in hike scenarios—pairs-trade opportunity (fade small caps vs. large caps).

- **Volatility behavior across scenarios** [51:07–53:57]: In hike scenarios, 30-day IV flattens coming into the event; in cut scenarios, IV gets more active; in hold scenarios, IV is muted. Post-event IV behavior differs: hikes → IV trickles down; cuts → IV rises; holds → IV does nothing. Standard FOMC days carry ~2 IV points surplus; other sessions trickle down ~1.25 points.

- **Straddle strategy for FOMC** [57:01–58:44]: Long volatility (straddle) into the statement is similar to earnings plays. At-the-money straddles are more fairly priced than wings (vs. long strangles). Theta decay in short-term options is aggressive; gamma scalping is necessary to survive the trade. Eric is experimenting with multiple expirations; primary focus is 2-day options due to better historical data.

- **Post-event retracement patterns** [55:03–56:32]: Retracements occur early and frequently; by 15 days post-event, retracements are nearly universal for both IV and price. Hikes show more contained 30-day IV change vs. cuts or holds. If a hike occurs, expect relatively compressed early moves; if a cut, expect larger price/IV moves.

- **Following-month directional bias** [01:01:11–01:02:02]: Hikes trend negative over the following month (tradable via short calls, short call spreads). Following day is "a literal shotgun blast" (high variance); following week tightens; following month tightens further. IV is high immediately around the event and decays afterward.

- **Equal-weighted vs. cap-weighted divergence** [01:03:02]: RSP (equal-weighted S&P) vs. SPY shows interesting back-and-forth early, then weakening in hike scenarios—another tradable effect.
