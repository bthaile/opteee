# GameStop Earnings and Options Analysis | Ep63

## Summary

A deep-dive into GameStop's current options structure, earnings, and strategic positioning. The session covers the extreme call skew (15+ volatility points), the mechanics of the covered-strangle strategy generating ~40% annualized returns, and the risks of buying long calls in a high-variance-risk-premium environment. Recent convertible-note exchange reduces dilution exposure but leaves ~$2.8B in remaining convertible obligations.

## Key takeaways

### Market context & GameStop positioning
- **Small-cap rally vs. large-cap stall** [19:33]: Small caps rallying while large caps oscillate sideways; rate-hike conversation impacts GameStop sensitivity.
- **Sector rotation headwinds** [21:10]: Financials, energy, healthcare leading; discretionary/retail sectors rolling off; GameStop typically correlates with underperforming cohorts.
- **Year-to-date performance** [22:32]: GME down ~5.4% YTD (from ~$22.60 to ~$19.16); covered-strangle up ~10% realized P&L—massive outperformance vs. buy-and-hold.

### Options structure & call skew
- **Extreme call skew** [24:09]: 1.2M call OI vs. low put OI (3–4× ratio); 30-DTE call IV 67.5% vs. put IV 52.3% = **15 volatility points of skew**—massive.
- **Why short calls dominate** [25:34]: Traders sell calls because they're richly priced; the market prices in tail-risk upside (potential for parabolic moves).
- **Delta profile reveals floor pricing** [30:26]–[34:08]: Plot strike vs. delta shows asymmetry: 4-point downside move (to $15) = 0.14 delta; 4-point upside move (to $23) = 2.36 delta. Market prices downside as very improbable; upside as plausible.

### Variance risk premium & the long-call trap
- **VRP definition** [43:09]: Implied volatility relative to realized volatility; GameStop consistently shows positive VRP (calls overpriced vs. realized moves).
- **The car-buying analogy** [44:48]: Buying calls is like paying $55k for a car you can only resell for $50k—you're overpaying for volatility.
- **Theoretical example** [47:05]–[50:37]: Stock moves 10% in your favor (23→25), but IV collapses 30 points (63%→33%); delta gain (+90 cents) offset by vega loss (−90 cents) = flat P&L despite correct directional call.
- **Mitigation strategies** [50:37]–[51:51]: Buy deep-ITM calls (90+ delta, minimal vega) or avoid long calls entirely; short premium is the edge.

### Covered-strangle mechanics & returns
- **Setup example** [37:41]–[39:09]: Hold shares; sell 20-delta calls (18 Sep expiry, sold Aug 3 for $0.18 and Aug 13 for $0.56); ROIC 5.74% and 3.7% over 46 and 36 days respectively = **46% and 38% annualized**.
- **Why it works** [40:34]: Call skew is so rich that you get paid lucratively just for holding shares and selling calls; short-put side is secondary (lower compensation).
- **Ratio-call-diagonal alternative** [40:34]: Sell calls at a ratio to preserve upside if GameStop runs; avoids leaving money on the table if assigned.
- **Comparison to index** [41:59]: Selling 30-delta put in index yields ~1% return; GameStop yields significantly more—the skew advantage.

### Earnings & balance sheet
- **Sales collapse, cost-cutting gains** [59:23]–[01:01:22]: Revenue declining; net income improving via expense reduction (not ideal long-term signal); COGS efficiency improving; operating expenses down.
- **Cash flow deterioration**: Concerning trend; company propped up by investment income (not core business).
- **EPS expansion without market response**: Market skeptical of sustainability; valuation play requires belief in RC turnaround *and* sufficient magnitude to justify opportunity cost.

### Convertible-note exchange & dilution
- **$1.4B exchange closed** [01:05:25]: Amended terms cap share issuance at 55.5M shares + $358M cash (replaces remaining stock consideration); eliminates contingent issues.
- **Remaining exposure**: ~$2.8B of convertible notes still outstanding; conversion probability remains low but non-zero.
- **Warrant mechanics** [01:09:56]: GME1 options embed 10 warrants per contract; warrants expire Oct 30, 2026 at 5 PM PT (before 2027 options revert to standard 100-share GME); warrant prices have cratered.

### Adjustment scenarios for assigned calls
- **Wait-and-see** [01:13:17]–[01:15:25]: Historical pattern: GameStop parabolic moves always retrace; ride out the pain if no fundamental catalyst.
- **Close at strike** [01:15:25]: Best adjustment window is when spot ≈ strike; gives flexibility to roll or unwind without locking in max loss.
- **LIFO accounting trick** [01:17:04]: Buy more shares at strike, use LIFO to exit assigned shares at scratch, keep premium, retain core position—adds risk but preserves upside.
- **Let assignment happen**: Accept call-away if move is temporary; re-enter if thesis intact.
