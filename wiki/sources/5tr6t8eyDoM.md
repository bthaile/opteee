# Why Market Gaps Will Ruin Your Risk Management

## Summary

Market gaps expose a critical flaw in pre-planned risk budgets for naked option strategies: when a security gaps overnight or during non-trading hours, your intended exit level may never be reached, forcing you to accept losses beyond your planned risk tolerance. The solution involves conservative position sizing for gap-prone instruments and strategic hedging using correlated liquid instruments (e.g., E-mini futures for SPX short strangles).

## Key takeaways

- **Gap risk breaks planned risk budgets** [00:00] — If you sell a short strangle expecting to manage risk at 2× credit received, a gap can force you to accept losses far exceeding that threshold because your exit price is never touched.
- **Naked strategies require gap-aware sizing** [00:00] — Position size more conservatively for instruments with high gap frequency; don't assume your risk-management rules will execute as planned.
- **Hedge with liquid correlated instruments** [00:00] — For after-hours gaps in products you cannot trade (e.g., SPX), use E-mini futures or other liquid proxies to hedge directional exposure when the underlying market is closed.

## Regime / context

This is a foundational risk-management principle applicable across all market regimes. Gap risk is particularly acute in overnight and weekend scenarios, and in products with limited or no after-hours trading (e.g., index options). The advice to hedge with E-mini futures assumes access to 24-hour futures markets and sufficient correlation with the underlying.
