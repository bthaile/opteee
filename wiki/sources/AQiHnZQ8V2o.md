# Return from Spine Surgery - Putting the Portfolio to Work | Stock Market Analysis

## Summary

Eric returns from spine surgery and reviews market action from Friday's selloff, focusing on rebuilding his portfolio with defined-risk positions. He analyzes tech weakness (particularly Google and AMD), identifies rebound opportunities in mega-cap names, and constructs three new trades: long call spreads in Google and Amazon, and a short put spread in FedEx. The session emphasizes portfolio-first positioning and volatility-term-structure analysis ahead of election week.

## Key takeaways

- **Post-surgery recovery & market re-engagement** [02:02–08:53]: Eric is recovering well from spine surgery but managing pain and mobility. He's re-entering trading after a week of reduced activity, prioritizing portfolio reconstruction.

- **Friday's market action & sector weakness** [10:10–11:36]: Nasdaq led the selloff; tech was problematic. Gold and silver fell unexpectedly. Commodities (corn, soybean, wheat) showed mixed signals; wheat remains weak. Bitcoin soft.

- **Google earnings rebound play** [14:19–19:21]: Google revenue +25% YoY, but EPS missed (212 vs. 301 expected) and net income fell. Eric views the selloff as overdone and constructs a long call spread: long 275 calls / short 295 calls (15 Jan expiry, ~0.69/0.40 delta, ~$8.80 debit, sized for ~1% risk on $100k account).

- **Amazon as sympathetic rebound** [32:09–34:58]: Amazon also sold off sympathetically. Eric builds a similar long call spread: long 240 calls / short 260 calls (15 Jan, ~0.56/0.34 delta, ~$8.40 debit). Targets upside with defined risk.

- **FedEx as uncorrelated upside** [34:58–37:48]: FedEx bucked the selloff and is approaching resistance near 330. Eric constructs a short put spread instead (different return profile): long 290 puts / short 300 puts (18 DTE, ~0.21/0.30 delta, ~$1.80 debit). Volatility risk premium is elevated (~75th percentile).

- **Volatility surface & election week setup** [25:40–27:46]: VIX term structure shows backwardation (1-day and 9-day elevated). 1 DTE straddle vol at 0.88% (85th percentile). Eric plans to sell premium (especially zero-DTE) into the elevated vol week. Election day typically offers vol-trading opportunities rather than directional bets.

- **Beginner advice: paper trading & buy-and-hold foundation** [11:36–14:19]: For new traders, Eric recommends starting with paper trading and running a parallel buy-and-hold portfolio. Most traders lack edge in their first 12–18 months; early profits often reflect luck, leading to overconfidence and drawdowns. Recommends *Options as a Strategic Investment* as foundational reading.
