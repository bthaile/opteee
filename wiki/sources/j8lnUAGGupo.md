### Evergreen mechanics

- **Covered strangle as core allocation tool** [15:36–18:48]. A covered strangle (long stock + short call + short put) is not a directional bet but a structure for capturing equity risk premium and variance risk premium simultaneously. It improves buy-and-hold or dollar-cost-averaging by overlaying a momentum factor; the host uses it in [[teq-q]], [[ibit]], and [[gme]]. Max profit occurs when the short call is deep in-the-money, not at the short strike.
- **Disconnect structure from expectation** [18:48–20:33]. A strangle's payoff profile is independent of trader expectation. Selling a strangle typically profits if price stays in range, but a covered strangle has long deltas and can profit even if the underlying rallies hard. Structure defines risk/reward; expectation is separate.
- **Premium collection ≠ dividend** [20:57–21:24]. Collecting option credit is fundamentally different from dividends: credits are tied to open positions with ongoing risk, whereas dividends are paid once and can be captured without holding through payout.
- **Elevated IV does not equal good premium-selling opportunity** [24:05–27:39]. High implied volatility percentile alone is insufficient; must also examine variance risk premium (VRP). High IVP + low VRP can signal mean reversion risk. Example: LQD had IVP 85 but VRP 7; QQQ had IVP 16 but VRP 71st percentile. Anchor decisions on both metrics, not just IV.
- **LEAP options (>80 delta) are structures, not strategies** [30:43–34:11]. High-delta long-dated calls are effectively stock replacement with slower theta/gamma decay. No inherent edge; utility depends on alignment with thesis and time horizon. If thesis is directional and long-term, structure is appropriate; if thesis is short-term, mismatch occurs.
- **Insider buying as signal; options chain confirmation** [39:55–46:43]. Insider buying is bullish but requires secondary confirmation. Examine options chains for unusual call/put imbalance, skew anomalies, volume-over-OI ratios, and institutional accumulation. Absence of anomalies suggests position is not yet consensus.
- **AI-assisted backtesting requires active scrutiny** [01:02:53–01:11:38]. AI can execute backtests faster but cannot replace researcher judgment. Must probe AI on methodology, cost assumptions, variable usage, and look-ahead bias. Offloading research to AI without verification is negligent; you own the results and the capital at risk. Use AI as accelerator, not replacement.
