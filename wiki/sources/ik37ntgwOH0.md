# Is Value Investing Still Relevant?

## Summary

An examination of value investing's historical performance and current relevance. The analysis traces the 80-year outperformance of cheap stocks over expensive ones (1926–2006), the peak valuation gap in February 2007, and the subsequent 43% drawdown in the value-versus-growth spread, raising questions about whether the traditional value premium persists in modern markets.

## Key takeaways

- **Historical dominance (1926–2006):** Value stocks (cheapest 30%) outperformed growth stocks (most expensive 30%) consistently over 80 years, with a 25× expansion of value relative to growth by February 2007 [00:00].
- **Peak and reversal:** The February 2007 peak marked the maximum value-to-growth spread; the current gap sits approximately 43% below that peak [00:00].
- **Regime shift:** The period from 2007 onward represents a structural break in the historical value premium, suggesting either a regime change or a fundamental challenge to the value thesis.

## Regime / context

This snapshot captures the post-2007 environment, where the traditional value premium has compressed significantly. The 43% drawdown from the 2007 peak suggests either mean reversion ahead or a permanent structural shift in market dynamics favoring growth and momentum over traditional value metrics. Further analysis would be needed to determine whether this represents a cyclical opportunity or a secular regime change.
