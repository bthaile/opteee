# Simplify Options Trading for Success

## Summary

Options trading success often hinges on simplification rather than complexity. The core insight is recognizing that implied volatility is not a single number per stock but a surface—each strike and term has its own vol. By identifying asymmetries in the volatility surface or term structure, traders can construct tradeable ideas based on relative mispricing rather than directional bets.

## Key takeaways

- **Simplification as strategy** [00:00] — Sometimes the best approach is to trade the underlying directly or use stock replacement via deep in-the-money calls rather than complex multi-leg structures.
- **Implied volatility is a surface, not a scalar** [00:22] — Each strike and each expiration term has its own implied volatility; there is no single "IV of the stock."
- **Volatility smile and skew as tradeable edges** [00:51] — Identifying relative mispricing between strikes (e.g., one option expensive relative to another) creates actionable trading ideas based on the shape of the volatility surface.
- **Reality-based position design** [00:00] — Position structure must be grounded in the actual market microstructure and volatility dynamics, not theoretical assumptions.

## Notable quotes

> "Sometimes with options, the best possible thing you could do is simplify."

> "There really is no like implied volatility of a stock. What I mean by that is there's a implied volatility surface. Each strike has its own vol. Each term has its own average vol."
